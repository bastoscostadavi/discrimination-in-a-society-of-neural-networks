\paragraph{Learning from unreliable sources.}
\todo{Dawid--Skene and the crowdsourcing lineage; modern annotator modelling;
per-annotator reliability in preference learning and reward modelling.
\emph{Connection to make:} the trust sector is a per-source reliability posterior
of exactly this kind. That literature asks whether reliability estimation recovers
the true labels; we ask what it does to the population of estimators, and find that
the same component that confers robustness is the one that transmits bias.}

\paragraph{Decentralised and federated learning.}
\todo{gossip and decentralised SGD; client reweighting; Byzantine-robust
aggregation. \emph{Connection to make:} this is where per-source weighting actually
ships. The distinction worth drawing is that peer weights there are usually a fixed
hyperparameter or the output of a robust aggregation rule, whereas here they are a
learned posterior with its own dynamics --- and it is the dynamics that produces the
transition. A robust-aggregation rule with static weights corresponds roughly to
ablation A1.}

\paragraph{Feedback loops in populations of learners.}
\todo{model collapse and self-consuming training loops; recommender feedback loops;
emergent conventions in multi-agent learning. \emph{Connection to make, and it must
be explicit:} model collapse is the nearest relative and a reviewer will raise it.
The distinction is that there the data distribution itself degrades under
recursive training, while here the task distribution is fixed and stationary
throughout --- the pathology is in how supervision is \emph{weighted}, not in what
is available to learn from.}
