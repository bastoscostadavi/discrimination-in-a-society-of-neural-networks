This paper studies how discriminatory structure can arise among interacting
learning agents, and two points bear stating.

The model is not a claim about people. Its agents are single-layer perceptrons,
its groups are arbitrary inert labels, and its ``tolerance'' is a single scalar.
We use the vocabulary of in-group and out-group because it names the structure
compactly, not because the model licenses inferences about human prejudice, whose
causes are historical, institutional and material in ways nothing here
represents. No human subjects are involved and no personal data is used. The
synthetic class labels are not proxies for any protected attribute, and results
about them should not be read as results about any real demographic group.

The practical direction of the work is defensive. The result implies that a
population of learning agents can occupy a discriminatory collective state while
every agent in it would pass an individual audit, and that the state becomes
invisible to precisely the evaluation protocol that is standard. That argues for
population-level measurement of deployed multi-agent systems, and the order
parameters used here need only a pairwise opinion alignment and a pairwise trust,
both of which are cheap to elicit.
